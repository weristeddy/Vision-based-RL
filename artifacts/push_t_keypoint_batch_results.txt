1. THE SEED REPLICATES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mean over the last 500 iterations, DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16, all on VisualSlow:

    arm                      yaw error         episode_success
    LIN  seed 0            0.582 +- 0.110            0.235
    LIN  seed 100          0.674 +- 0.111            0.213
    KEYPT seed 0           0.788 +- 0.129            0.098
    LIN kl0.15 seed 0      0.882 +- 0.121            0.043
    maniskill seed 0       1.082 +- 0.136            0.007

The two LIN seeds differ by 0.092, less than one within-run standard deviation.
This is a real effect, not a lucky seed. 21-24% episode success at the 0.90
overlap threshold against a full-circle goal, versus 0.7% for the same
curriculum with ManiSkill's own reward -- a 30x gap from the orientation factor
alone.

Also resolved: the caveat I attached to the first read. The seed-0 run was at
0.600 mid-curriculum and I warned it could degrade once the circle opened at
iteration 4750. It did degrade, from 0.263 at iteration 3000, but it stabilised
rather than collapsing:

    iteration          3000   4000   4500   4750   5000   5500   5990
    LIN seed 0         0.263  0.444  0.562  0.624  0.698  0.766  0.622
    LIN seed 100       0.266  0.523  0.486  0.572  0.746  0.844  0.631
    maniskill seed 0   0.591  0.817  0.909  0.885  0.989  1.117  1.295

That last row is the finding. With ManiSkill's factor the curriculum's gains are
destroyed as the range opens -- 0.591 to 1.295, back to chance -- which is
exactly the collapse recorded for the coarse 45-degree schedule, now reproduced
at 22.5 degrees. The linear factor is what makes the gains survive the full
circle. Curriculum and reward shape are not independent levers here: the
curriculum gets the policy to a good place and the factor is what lets it stay.


2. THE KEYPOINT REWARD DOES NOT HELP -- my idea, and it did not work
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both arms answer cleanly.

With the curriculum, keypoint is worse than linear: 0.788 vs 0.582 yaw, 9.8% vs
23.5% success. The gap is about 2 within-run standard deviations, so this is not
noise.

Without the curriculum it does nothing at all. VisualGoal KEYPT scores 1.378 on
DinoV2, and the three existing VisualGoal baselines on that architecture span
1.349 to 1.589. It is inside the baseline band.

So the diagnosis that motivated it -- that the position/orientation split is
what blocks orientation learning -- is not supported. Removing the split did not
help; sharpening the orientation factor's gradient (linear) did. The competing
gradients were apparently not the binding constraint.

Two candidate reasons, neither tested: the non-monotonicity in yaw that I
measured before submitting (sign changes at 43 and 133 degrees at a 10 cm
offset) may cost more than the removed dead zone is worth; and merging the terms
fixes the position/orientation ratio at whatever the T's geometry implies, which
removes a knob rather than setting it well.

Keeping the code -- it is tested, cheap, and the negative result is worth having
recorded -- but I would not spend more GPUs on it without a different kernel.


3. THE UPDATE BUDGET WAS NOT THE BOTTLENECK -- also my idea, also wrong
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The mechanism worked exactly as predicted: raising desired_kl from 0.05 to 0.15
raised realised minibatch updates per iteration from 29 to 39 on DinoV2 and from
33 to 77 on CompactVit. So the early stop really was the thing limiting updates.

The outcome went the wrong way. DinoV2 fell from 0.582 to 0.882 yaw and from
23.5% to 4.3% success. CompactVit, which got the largest budget increase (77 of
128, more than double), is the worst run in the batch on overlap (0.233) and
position (0.073) -- and it is still running at 5434, so treat that one as
provisional.

Reading it back: 8 epochs at desired_kl 0.05 realising ~2 epochs is not a
misconfiguration that was costing performance. It is a working trust region, and
the config's stated 8 epochs is better understood as a ceiling the early stop is
supposed to cut. I was wrong to call it mis-set.


4. STILL ONE ARCHITECTURE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CompactVit-Flatten fails in all four new arms at 1.52-1.57, and failed at 1.514
on the original seed. Five arms, two seeds, no movement. The architecture, not
the seed and not the reward.

What has never been tried is this arm on the encoders already known to be strong
on this task -- DinoV2-Afa6 and the SpatialSoftmax rows. The reason CompactVit
and LocalGrid16 were picked was that success on a weak pair implies success on a
good one; half that bet has now paid. The obvious next 4 jobs:

  bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualSlow --profile b200 \
    --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-Afa6,DinoV2ViTS14-SpatialSoftmax" \
    -- --orientation-reward linear \
       --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

  bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualSlow --profile b200 \
    --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-Afa6,DinoV2ViTS14-SpatialSoftmax" \
    -- --orientation-reward linear --agent.seed 100 \
       --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

Beyond that, the two ideas with support behind them and no run yet are the
success-gated separation curriculum (arXiv:2607.10892 gates on 50 successes per
stage; ours is a wall clock) and a goal-relative observation/action frame, which
is the larger of the two gaps to that paper and not a flag-sized change.


APPENDIX: HAS CompactVit-Flatten EVER LEARNED ORIENTATION?
====================================================================================================
Swept every Push-T run in the project carrying the CompactVit-Flatten tag -- 30
runs across SlowGoal, Curriculum, Uniform, UniformQuad, FreeStart, SlowFree,
NearGoal, GrowStart, FrontCam, VisualGoal, VisualFree, VisualGrow, VisualSlow,
and all four reward shapes.

Answer: no. Not fully, and not partially in any useful sense.

Measured as the best 200-iteration rolling mean of yaw error at any point in any
run -- single logged points are noise and give a misleadingly good number, so
they are not used here -- the best CompactVit-Flatten has ever achieved is:

    1.199 rad = 68.7 deg     PushT-SlowGoal, maniskill reward, iteration 2725
                             episode_success 0.004   overlap 0.334

Chance is pi/2 = 1.571 rad = 90 deg. So the best moment in the architecture's
entire history is 21 degrees below chance with a 0.4% success rate. Every one of
the 30 runs sits at 1.19 or above; most sit at 1.45-1.52.

On the specific question of the pinned phase: correct, it does not learn it
there either. That 1.199 IS the pinned-goal phase -- SlowGoal holds the goal yaw
fixed for the first 3,000 iterations and the best rolling mean inside the pin,
across every SlowGoal and VisualSlow run, is that same 1.199. The current arm's
CompactVit runs reach only 1.369 and 1.378 during the pin before returning to
1.51-1.54.

The same run's trajectory shows what it is doing instead:

    at its best-yaw moment (it 2725)   yaw 1.230   success 0.004   overlap 0.334
    final 500 iterations               yaw 1.362   success 0.046   overlap 0.448

Success and overlap improve while yaw error gets worse. It is learning to
transport the T and ignoring the goal orientation -- the failure mode this whole
campaign is about, except that for this encoder no intervention has moved it at
all.

One correction to the sweep: the single best row initially looked like a 1.191,
but that run is tagged CompactVit-**FlattenRelu**, a different adapter, and it
collapsed to overlap 0.000 by the end. It is not this architecture.

Implication. Five arms, two seeds and thirteen variants have now failed on this
encoder while the same interventions work on DinoV2-LocalGrid16. It is not the
reward, the curriculum, the seed or the goal visibility. A 128-dim scratch ViT
with a plain flatten head is not extracting object yaw from these images at all,
and there is no reason to keep it as the second architecture in this comparison.
Replace it with DinoV2-Afa6 and DinoV2-SpatialSoftmax, per the commands above.
