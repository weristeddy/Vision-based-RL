PUSH-T ORIENTATION -- WHAT TO SUBMIT NEXT
====================================================================================================

Read the results file first: artifacts/push_t_batch_results.txt. One correction
to the caveat I put there. The goal-yaw curriculum narrows the *goal* range but
the object's initial yaw stays uniform, and wrapping a uniform angle against
anything is still uniform -- so chance yaw error is pi/2 = 1.571 at every rung.
VisualSlow's mid-run numbers are measured against the same chance level as the
finished runs. The task is easier (one canonical target orientation instead of a
conditional mapping), but the metric is not inflated.


1. NEW SEED OF THE ONE ARM THAT MOVED                                  2 jobs
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

VisualSlow + linear on DinoV2-LocalGrid16 reached yaw 0.600 with at_goal 0.042,
the only non-zero at_goal among the fresh runs. One seed. The w=0.60 pair
differed by 0.245 vs 0.000 on the same config, so a single seed proves very
little here.

Seed 100, not 1: run_train sets `seed = cfg.agent.seed + rank`, so on a
four-GPU profile seed 1 reuses three of seed 0's four rank seeds.

  bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualSlow --profile b200 \
    --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
    -- --orientation-reward linear --agent.seed 100 \
       --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000


2. KEYPOINT REWARD -- the one structural idea left                     2 or 4 jobs
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Added in this change as `--orientation-reward keypoint`. Every shape tried so
far scores position and orientation separately and trades them with
`orientation_weight`. That split is the measured failure: the two factors have
independent gradients, so there is a distance at which a push that correctly
reduces yaw error still loses reward (-0.00101 at 15 cm and 162 degrees under
ManiSkill's shape), and no value of the weight removes the competition -- w=0.5,
0.65 and 0.8 have all now been run, and all three failed the same way.

Keypoints remove the split instead of retuning it. Four points on the T are
carried to where the goal pose would put them, and the reward is the mean of
(1 - tanh(5 d))^2 over the four distances -- the same shape the position factor
already uses. The points are the two ends of each box's long axis, computed from
FOOTPRINT_PARTS rather than typed in, so they cannot drift from the shape the
overlap rasteriser scores:

    crossbar -x end   (-60.0, -22.5) mm    64.1 mm from centre
    crossbar +x end   (+60.0, -22.5) mm    64.1 mm
    stem inner end    (  0.0,  -7.5) mm     7.5 mm
    stem outer tip    (  0.0, +82.5) mm    82.5 mm

Three of them swing hard under rotation; the stem's inner end sits 7.5 mm from
the centre and so contributes almost pure position signal. Non-collinear, which
is what makes the four distances pin SE(2) rather than position alone.

Provenance, since you asked and it matters: the quantity is established, the
shaping is mine. Averaging ||R1 p + t1 - (R2 p + t2)|| over model points is the
ADD metric of Hinterstoisser et al. (ACCV 2012), the standard 6-DoF pose error
in the LineMOD/BOP line; specifying a manipulation target through keypoints
instead of a pose is kPAM (Manuelli et al., ISRR 2019). Using it as a dense RL
reward is common in manipulation. But this is not a transcription of any one
paper's reward -- the four points, the tanh and the scale are chosen here to
match the factor being replaced. A translation
moves all four points, a rotation moves them in opposing directions, so both are
one currency and the relative worth of position and orientation is set by the
T's geometry (16.5 cm of stem-tip travel for a half turn) rather than by a
hyperparameter. `--orientation-weight` has no effect with this shape.

Honest limits, both checked rather than assumed. See artifacts/push_t_keypoints.png.

(a) It is *not* flat nowhere. Under a pure rotation about the object's centre
    every keypoint distance is 2 r sin(e/2), stationary at pi, and the crossbar
    terms also cancel when the position offset lies along the T's mirror axis.
    What changes is the order. Gradient as a fraction of each shape's own peak,
    T on the goal:

              yaw error    keypoint    maniskill
                170 deg    2.4e-02     2.0e-03
                175 deg    1.2e-02     2.5e-04
                179 deg    2.3e-03     1.9e-06

    ManiSkill is three decades flatter by 179 degrees. And 10 cm off across the
    mirror axis the keypoint ratio at 179 degrees is 0.64 -- no dead zone at
    all. Episodes are off-position nearly all the time.

(b) Off-position the reward is no longer monotone in yaw alone. At a 10 cm
    offset it turns at 43 and 133 degrees, so there are configurations where
    rotating away from the goal orientation pays. That is correct for a joint
    SE(2) error -- the T must travel as well as turn and the metric prices both
    -- but it is a real difference from the decomposed shapes, which are
    monotone in yaw everywhere by construction. It is the one thing I would
    watch if this arm underperforms.

  bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualSlow --profile b200 \
    --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
    -- --orientation-reward keypoint \
       --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

Optional 2 more, on VisualGoal, which has no curriculum -- this is the arm that
says whether the reward alone is enough or only helps the curriculum along:

  bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualGoal --profile b200 \
    --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \

3. THE HYPERPARAMETER ARM -- and it is not exploration                 2 jobs
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pulled Policy/mean_std and Loss/performed_updates for all 31 runs since
2026-09-04. Two things came out, and only one of them is a problem.

Exploration is NOT the bottleneck. Action std starts at 0.6065 and decays to
0.15-0.26 in every run, and the *successful* runs collapse it fastest:

  VisualGoal DinoV2-Afa6   (yaw 0.236, works)  std 0.285 by it 274, floor 0.150 from it 4446
  VisualGoal threshCURR D  (yaw 1.319, fails)  std 0.500 at it 274, reaches 0.150 only at 5447
  VisualSlow LINEAR D      (yaw 0.575, best)   std 0.156 at it 4468

Low std correlates with success, not failure. Raising entropy_coef or the std
floor is not indicated by this data.

The update budget IS a problem. num_learning_epochs=8 x num_mini_batches=16 =
128 minibatch updates per iteration. Measured mean over the last 200 iterations
of every run: 25 to 40. From iteration ~400 on, essentially every run performs
32 -- exactly 2 epochs. `early_stop_kl=True` with `desired_kl=0.05` aborts the
rest of the iteration the first time a single minibatch crosses 0.05, and it
does so in the second epoch, every time, in all 31 runs. clip_fraction sits at
0.27-0.31, well above the 0.1-0.2 a healthy PPO shows.

So the configured recipe is 8 epochs and the realised recipe is 2, and 75% of
every rollout's gradient compute is thrown away. That has been true of every
Push-T run in this project, including the ones that worked -- which is why I am
not claiming it is *the* reason orientation fails, only that it is the one
hyperparameter with clear evidence of being mis-set. schedule is "fixed" here,
so desired_kl is purely the early-stop threshold and nothing else moves.

  bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualSlow --profile b200 \
    --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
    -- --orientation-reward linear --agent.algorithm.desired-kl 0.15 \
       --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

WHAT I AM NOT PROPOSING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dense overlap as the reward. The rasteriser already computes it every step and
it is monotone in yaw near alignment, but it is exactly zero whenever the two
footprints are disjoint -- which is most of an episode -- so it needs a position
term alongside it and reintroduces the split the keypoint reward removes.

A state-dependent orientation weight, w(d) = w_max * exp(-d / sigma), so
orientation only pays once the T is near the goal. It is a real idea and it is
cheap, but it is the same two-factor competition with a moving crossover, and
the keypoint reward subsumes it.
