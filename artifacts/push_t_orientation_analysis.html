Push-T · 51 runs · goal-yaw curriculum

The T never turns

Across 48 image-based runs, only four learned to orient the block at all — and every one of them had a long fixed-goal phase. The curriculum is real, but it is compensating for a reward whose orientation gradient vanishes exactly where the block starts.

What the runs say

One axis explains almost everything

Chance level for mean absolute yaw error under a uniform goal yaw is π/2 = 1.571 rad. Read the table with that number in mind: nearly every cell sits on it.

The fixed phase is the active ingredient

NatureCNN-SpatialSoftmax is the clean experiment. Identical architecture, identical reward, identical camera — only the fixed-goal phase differs. With 500 iterations it ends at 1.75 rad, worse than it started. With 3,000 it reaches 0.59 before widening even begins.

500 iterations is before anything exists

At the moment the short curriculum starts widening, no architecture except DINOv2-SpatialSoftmax has moved off chance. The rungs then erode competence that was never there.

Widening can actively destroy

R3M-L3-SpatialSoftmax reaches 1.10 rad by the end of its fixed phase, then decays to 1.55 — back to chance — as the goal range opens. Partial competence is not robust to the next rung.

Capacity is not the binding constraint

NatureCNN-SS reaches 0.69 under a long fixed phase and 1.75 under a short one; DINOv2-LocalGrid16 goes 0.59 against 1.57. The same networks both succeed and fail, so the encoder is not what decides it — it decides how much easy-phase time is needed. Given privileged state the task is solved in 500 iterations.

Learning curves

Where orientation forms, and where it doesn’t

Two things stand out. First, the descent is slow — NatureCNN takes roughly 2,000 iterations of a fixed goal before yaw error breaks below 1.0. Second, DINOv2-SpatialSoftmax under the short curriculum descends fast and then oscillates between 0.40 and 0.69 rather than consolidating: each new rung knocks it back.

Verified in the renderer

What the encoder is actually given

The metrics above say orientation is not being learned. These are the frames it would have to be learned from, rendered from both views at the same six episodes.

Two problems are visible, and both measure larger than expected.

The gripper hides half the block during contact

Measured on the fixed-colour scene so the T is segmentable by its own red. With the end-effector 6–10 cm from the block, the visible silhouette drops to 51% of its far-field area in external_front, against 84% in external. It is never fully hidden in either view — but the front view loses half the shape at exactly the moment a corrective push is being chosen.

A quarter of episodes give the T no contrast

object_color samples uniform over the whole RGB cube; the table independently gets an arbitrary AmbientCG photograph. Nothing couples them. Over the 1,203-texture pool, 24% of resets land the block within a 1.2:1 luminance ratio of its table and 50% within 1.5:1 — isoluminant, separable by hue alone, while the lighting randomization is simultaneously varying diffuse, specular, and ambient colour.

Neither of these is a curriculum problem or a reward problem. A policy cannot regress an orientation it cannot see, and a fixed-goal phase does not help if a quarter of the episodes in it are unreadable.

Camera geometry, swept

Tilting the front view fixes most of the occlusion

The shipped external_front_cam sits 75° down — near overhead — at 0.79 m. Its XML comment says the pose was chosen because “a gripper pressed down beside the T foreshortens away from this camera instead of across it.” Measured, the opposite holds: from near overhead, anything above the block covers it. Sweeping elevation at a fixed look-at point:

45° at 0.70 m is the best pose in the sweep on every axis at once: the largest projected T of anything tested (2.22%, against 1.87% shipped and 1.25% for external), two and a half times the visible area during contact, and less wasted background than 45° from further back. Its position is (0.845, 0, 0.508) with xyaxes="0 1 0 -0.7071 0 0.7071".

Going below 45° costs projected area and adds background without improving occlusion. Note the contrast problem is orthogonal and survives the change — the pale block on wood and the teal block on teal are both still unreadable in the bottom row.

The mechanism

The orientation reward has a dead zone

The registered dense reward is ManiSkill3’s, normalized by 3. Its three terms:

r = ((cos e + 1) / 2)² / 2   ← orientation, caps at 0.5
  + (1 − tanh(5 d))² / 2   ← position, caps at 0.5
  + √(1 − tanh(5 dₜ₄₃ₚ)) / 20   ← reach, caps at 0.05
  → replaced by 3.0 when at_goal

Differentiate the orientation term and the problem is immediate.

Squaring the cosine is what does it: at 138° the gradient is 0.044, seven times smaller than at its peak; at 172° it is 0.0004. A badly misoriented block gets almost no signal telling it to turn. That is precisely the state a fixed-goal phase avoids — which is why the curriculum works at all, and why it is a workaround rather than a fix.

The second half of the problem is that ignoring orientation pays well. Solving position alone and leaving yaw at 90° earns 64% of the achievable dense reward; at 180° it still earns 52%. The terms are additive, so position-only is a broad, comfortable local optimum — and the observed signature of every stalled run is exactly that: overlap climbing to 0.40–0.47 while yaw error sits on chance.

The sparse bonus that would break the tie is worth 2.9× the dense maximum, but it fires in only 4 of 51 runs. At the 0.98 overlap threshold it demands roughly 2 mm or 2.5° and is effectively unreachable; even at 0.90 it stays near zero.

The degeneracy

The fixed-goal phase teaches the wrong policy

In PushTCommand._resample_command the block’s initial yaw and the goal yaw are drawn independently, on the same reset. While the curriculum holds target_yaw_range at (0, 0), the goal is a constant — so the optimal policy is rotate the block to absolute yaw zero, and the target_pose observation carries no information at all. Nothing forces the policy to read it.

So the 3,000 iterations SlowGoal spends before its first rung are not spent learning orientation. They are spent learning one orientation, with the goal input ignorable. When widening starts, the policy must suddenly begin using an observation it has had every incentive to discard, and the competence it built does not transfer. That is the erosion in R3M-L3 and the oscillation in DINOv2-SS, and it is why a longer fixed phase buys a better start but not a better finish.

The fix keeps everything that made the fixed phase work — small yaw errors, so the reward gradient is in its steep region — without the degeneracy: curriculum the initial relative yaw, not the goal. Leave target_yaw_range at the full circle from step one and sample the block’s starting yaw near the goal:

object_yaw = wrap_to_pi(target_yaw + U(−half, half)) # half: 0 → π

Both quantities are already sampled in the same function on the same env_ids, so this is a two-line change. Every episode now requires reading the goal, the early ones only ask for a small correction, and there is no canonical solution to unlearn.

Round two · 15 runs, 2026-08-19

The fix failed, and the reason is still open

All fifteen RelativeYaw runs completed 3,000 iterations. All fifteen finished on rung 0 — the curriculum never advanced once — and overlap collapsed to 0.00–0.18 against 0.40–0.76 in the generations it replaced. Fourteen learned to leave the object untouched: reward flat at 135 from iteration 50 to 3,000, object–goal distance unchanged at 0.23 m, contact penalty never firing.

The leading hypothesis was an interaction between two of the four changes. Value of one clumsy early push — 1 cm nearer the goal, 0.1 rad of unwanted spin — with the object starting perfectly oriented, as rung 0 guarantees:

The flat spot at zero error was load-bearing. A constant gradient punishes every clumsy first contact, because at rung 0 the orientation can only get worse. The curriculum then placed every episode at exactly the angle where the new reward is least forgiving, and a do-nothing policy paying 0.537 of maximum became a trap that fourteen of fifteen runs never left.

A quadratic term inverts ManiSkill's curve instead of flattening it: no gradient where the object is already right, twice ManiSkill's peak gradient where it is most wrong. It keeps the property that made the fix necessary and restores the one that made training start.

This hypothesis is not confirmed

Four short workstation runs — the committed reward, the committed reward without the curriculum, and ManiSkill's reward with and without it — all produced overlap 0.0000 and a flat reward, including the ManiSkill-plus-uniform-yaw configuration that reached 0.40–0.76 overlap on the cluster. The positive control failed identically, so the test discriminated nothing.

The budget was the error: 120 iterations at 256 environments is about 0.5 M environment steps, while SlowGoal first moved off zero overlap at iteration 500 of 4,096 environments — roughly 33 M steps, some 64 times more. Below that threshold nothing pushes, whatever the reward.

So the sign-of-the-gradient argument above remains a plausible mechanism and nothing more. What is established is only the matched cluster comparison: the four-change bundle fails where the previous generation succeeded. Which of the four is responsible needs a one-at-a-time ablation from the working baseline, at cluster scale, around 800 iterations per job.

Two secondary faults compounded it. The gate needed 50% success to advance, and success requires 0.90 overlap — the one run that escaped and pushed well reached 0.610 overlap and 0.147 success, so it would not have advanced either. And nothing detected the stall: fifteen jobs held sixty B200s while reporting rung 0 from iteration 50 onward.

What the round does establish: the environment, camera, fixed-colour object and reward optimum are all sound. NatureCNN-SpatialSoftmax broke out at iteration 1,900 and reached overlap 0.610 with 9 mm position error — better placement than any earlier generation — and its reward rose from the 135 plateau to 193. The target is reachable and pays far better. Only the basin of attraction at the start was wrong.

Round three · ablation, 2026-08-20

It was the curriculum, not the reward

Ten runs at 800 iterations: four arms plus the baseline at the same budget, on the two architectures that answer the question. Each arm is SlowGoal with exactly one change.

The reward hypothesis is falsified. AddShaping carries the shaped reward with the quadratic orientation term on SlowGoal's curriculum, and it pushes fine. Every attempt to fix this generation by reshaping the orientation term was aimed at the wrong component.

The relative-yaw curriculum is flawed at its easy end, and reward shaping cannot fix it. Rung 0 starts the object already at the goal orientation, so under any reward that decreases with yaw error — ManiSkill's included, as this arm shows — every action can only make the orientation term worse. Leaving the object alone is optimal and the gate can never advance.

The goal-yaw curriculum's easy end differs in kind: the goal is pinned but the object's initial yaw stays uniform, so it remains a full rotation task with one target to learn and rotating pays from the first episode. The original objection to it — a pinned goal makes the goal observation uninformative — still stands, but the cure has to come from somewhere other than removing the orientation error: a looser success threshold, or a shorter push, not an already-solved rotation.

Camera and fixed object colour are both safe, and on NatureCNN clearly good: overlap 0.149 and 0.228 against the baseline's 0.090, and the red object is the only arm in which NatureCNN learned any orientation at 800 iterations (yaw 0.350 against 1.310). DINOv2 sits slightly below baseline on all four arms, which at one seed and 800 iterations is not separable from noise.

Round four · 15 runs, 2026-08-20

The perception fixes worked

Same 15 architectures, same 6,000 iterations, same goal-yaw curriculum. Changed: the tilted camera, the fixed red object, and 22.5° rungs instead of 45°.

NatureCNN-Flatten went from chance to 79% success — 1.516 rad and 0.010 success, to 0.113 rad and 0.794. It is now the best result in the project, and the plainest architecture in the table: a three-layer CNN with no adapter at all.

That is a reversal. DINOv2-SpatialSoftmax, previously the only arm that worked, is flat at 0.47 while two scratch encoders pass 0.78. Frozen features cannot adapt to a new viewpoint and a new object colour; a trainable trunk can. The comparison the whole sweep exists to make now has a clear answer, and it is not the one the pretrained-representation literature would predict.

The schedule change was not what did it. Measured across the widening window again, the finer rungs degraded 9 of 15 against the coarse version's 8 — no better. What improved is the level competence reached during the fixed phase: the top runs now sit at 0.03–0.2 rad by iteration 3,000 where they were at 0.1–1.5. The camera and the object colour did that; halving the rungs did nothing measurable.

Which relocates the bottleneck. Fixed-phase competence is now excellent and widening still costs. DinoV2-Afa6 is the extreme case, twice over: 0.030 rad at iteration 3,000 — 1.7 degrees, solved — collapsing to 1.859 by the end, with final overlap of exactly zero. The policy learns one canonical orientation and cannot carry it to others.

That is the goal-representation defect identified three rounds ago and never addressed. While the goal yaw is pinned, the target_pose observation is constant, so it is formally uninformative and the network has every reason to ignore it; when it starts varying there is no learned pathway from that input to behaviour. Rendering the goal into the image is what removes the defect, because a policy that learns “align the object with the visible target” learns a relation that still holds when the target turns.

Round five · 45 runs, no curriculum

Viable, and unreliable

Three sweeps of fifteen: the curriculum removed, with ManiSkill's reward and with a quadratic orientation term, and the latter again with the control-rate gamma. Yaw error and success at 6,000 iterations.

The best policy in the project has no curriculum. NatureCNN-SpatialSoftmax with the quadratic reward and gamma 0.996 reaches 0.078 rad and 0.859 success, beating the curriculum's best of 0.113 and 0.794. So the answer to whether direct full-circle training can work is yes, and it can work better.

But it works rarely. The outcomes are bimodal — every run lands either near 0.1 rad or in a band from 1.1 to 1.75, with nothing between. It is an escape event, not a learning curve, and the escape rate is 1 and 2 of fifteen against the curriculum's 8. The curriculum does not raise the ceiling; it raises the probability of reaching it, from roughly a tenth to a half.

DINOv2-Afa6 is the exception that is not luck: it escapes in both no-curriculum arms, at 0.158 and 0.112, and beats its own curriculum result of 0.288. This is the architecture the curriculum's widening previously destroyed — 0.030 rad on the pinned goal collapsing to 1.859. For this one architecture the curriculum is not merely unnecessary, it is harmful.

The quadratic reward is not carrying the result. The control arm was submitted with 600 iterations instead of 6,000, so the matched comparison at full budget is missing. At the 600 iterations both arms do share, the quadratic term is better in 3 architectures, worse in 7, level in 5, with means of 1.511 against 1.541 — no benefit. That window closes before any escape occurs, so it is weak evidence, but it is not support, and the hypothesis that the dead zone was the obstacle remains unconfirmed.

One run is worth watching for a different reason. NatureCNN-SpatialSoftmax without gamma ends at 1.216 while still descending — 1.424 at 2,000, 1.211 at 5,000. It is not stuck at chance, it is escaping too slowly to finish. The 6,000-iteration budget may be truncating this arm rather than bounding it.

What to change

Five changes, cheapest first

All direct RL: no auxiliary losses, no distillation, and no overlap reward. Sized to fit inside a 3,000-iteration budget, which at 4,096 environments is already 197 M environment steps and roughly 790,000 episodes — ample data. Nothing here asks for a longer run.

  1. Move the front camera to 45° and 0.70 m

    do first~2 hours

    Two numbers in the XML: pos="0.845 0 0.508" and xyaxes="0 1 0 -0.7071 0 0.7071". It more than doubles the visible silhouette during contact, gives the largest projected T of any pose tested, adds shading relief, and wastes less of the frame than the alternatives. Register it as a new camera view rather than editing external_front, so the fifteen SlowGoal runs stay reproducible.

  2. Fix the block colour — but not to red

    do first~1 hour

    Fixing the colour is the right call for isolating the learning problem, and it removes the 24% of resets where a uniform-cube sample lands isoluminant with the table. Red is a defensible choice but it is not the best one: against the 1,203-texture pool, the current push_t_red leaves 3.0% of tables hard on both luminance and hue at once, and 25.6% hard on luminance alone. Magenta and cyan leave 0.0% and 0.5%, because no photographic texture is magenta or cyan. White is the worst bright option at 7.5%, since most of the pool is light. Black scores perfectly on the table but collides with the robot, which is also black.

    Worth being explicit that this trades sim-to-real colour robustness for a clean signal. When you come back to randomizing, condition the draw on the sampled table instead of removing it — resample until the luminance ratio clears about 2:1 — which keeps full coverage without the invisible quarter.

  3. Curriculum the initial relative yaw, not the goal

    highest payoff~half day

    The change described above: keep the goal uniform on the full circle and widen the block’s starting offset from it. This is what makes a curriculum that finishes inside 3,000 iterations plausible, because no part of the budget goes into a goal-blind policy that later has to be undone.

    Gate the rungs on performance rather than a step counter, with hysteresis so a regression steps back, and use increments smaller than π/4 — the erosion and oscillation in the current curves both say the jumps are too coarse. goal_yaw_curriculum already re-reads its command config on every resample, so the machinery exists.

    And treat the criterion as a diagnostic, not just a gate: if relative yaw error has not come down by a few hundred iterations at a small offset, the configuration is broken and no amount of extra training will fix it. That is the check that tells you quickly, instead of six thousand iterations later.

  4. Reshape the orientation term, keeping the ManiSkill form

    high payoff~half day

    The dead zone is caused by the square, not by the additive structure: at 138° the gradient is already seven times below its peak and at 172° it is a thousandth of it. Dropping the square — (cos e + 1) / 4, same 0.5 cap — or using an angle-linear term 0.5 · (1 − |e| / π) keeps a usable gradient all the way to 180°.

    Separately, multiplying the orientation and position factors rather than adding them removes the position-only optimum that currently pays 64% of the achievable dense reward for solving half the task. Both are small edits to maniskill_dense_reward and neither requires the overlap metric. Register as a new variant so test_push_t_reward_exactly_matches_maniskill_normalized_dense_formula keeps passing for the existing tasks.

  5. Anneal the success threshold

    medium payoff~half day

    A bonus worth 2.9× the dense maximum that fires in 4 of 51 runs is shaping nothing. Start near 0.6, tighten toward 0.90 as success climbs, on the same criterion-gated machinery as the yaw rungs. Or replace the binary cliff with a graded bonus above 0.7 overlap — which uses overlap to shape the bonus, not as the dense reward.

Before you cite any of this

What the comparison cannot yet support

  • n = 1 per cell. There is no seed replication anywhere. Two nominally similar no-curriculum DINOv2-SpatialSoftmax runs differ by 1.27 rad — larger than the entire effect attributed to the curriculum. Three seeds on the top three configurations should come before any claim goes in the thesis.
  • SlowGoal had twice the compute. 6,000 iterations against 3,000. Some of its advantage is simply more training; the comparison at matched iteration 3,000 is the honest one, and there SlowGoal’s DINOv2-SS is roughly level with Curriculum’s.
  • The variants differ on four axes at once — camera, scene, success threshold, and curriculum. “No curriculum vs curriculum” is not isolated by any pair of variants currently run.
  • Run names disagree with run tags. The batch named “… - Default” carries the PushT-FrontCam task tag, and the batch named “… - Curriculum” carries PushT-SlowGoal. Every number here is grouped by task tag, not by name. Worth fixing at the source before the next sweep.
