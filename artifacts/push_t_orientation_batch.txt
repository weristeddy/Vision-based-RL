Push-T orientation batch -- six jobs
====================================

The hypothesis. Every one of the 54 GrowStart / NearGoal / FreeStart / SlowFree
/ VisualFree runs used `maniskill_dense`, whose orientation factor
((cos e + 1)/2)^2 has ZERO gradient at e = pi. 23% of a uniform goal yaw sits
where that gradient is under a tenth of its own peak, and failed episodes have a
mean goal yaw of 135 degrees against 78 for solved ones. So the start-state
family was judged with the orientation signal switched off exactly where it
fails. `linear` (1 - |e|/pi) has full gradient everywhere.

All six carry the drawn goal marker and full colour DR on both the T and the
marker, which is what VisualFree and VisualGrow give.

Chance is pi/2 = 1.571 rad. Every run in this family sits at 1.0-1.8, so
"did orientation get learned" reads straight off yaw_error without a baseline.

Shared flags: gamma 0.996 and 6000 iterations reproduce the settings the whole
Push-T generation was trained with; the registered defaults are 0.99 and 3000.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PAIR 1 -- NearGoal geometry, linear orientation reward.  2 jobs.

VisualFree is FreeStart geometry plus the marker and colour DR, and NearGoal is
FreeStart plus near_goal_probability 0.25, so this is a flag away. A quarter of
episodes start 6-15 mm out with 5-20 degrees of yaw error -- in the endgame,
which is where maniskill still has gradient and where w=0.5 punishes rotation.

bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualFree --profile b200 \
  --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
  -- --orientation-reward linear --near-goal-probability 0.25 \
     --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PAIR 2 -- GrowStart geometry, linear orientation reward.  2 jobs.

VisualGrow is new: VisualFree plus GrowStart's reverse separation curriculum.
Every episode starts within 5 cm of its goal and the cap grows to the target
range's diagonal, ending at VisualFree's distribution exactly.

bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualGrow --profile b200 \
  --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
  -- --orientation-reward linear \
     --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

PAIR 3 -- resume two of the colour-DR batch at a higher orientation weight.  2 jobs.

Same variant, same colour DR, same marker: the weight is the only change. These
policies already transport, and at 2 cm a correct rotation scores -0.00127 per
step under the 0.5 split against +0.00331 at 0.8, so they are sitting in exactly
the regime where w=0.5 punishes what they still have to learn. A fresh run pays
a transport cost for a high weight; these have already paid it.

  arch                          run        pos     yaw    std
  DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16      t7exp2zq  0.017  1.516  0.250
  NatureCnn-SpatialSoftmax      2mt3uvag  0.036  1.587  0.184

Why these two. LocalGrid16 positions best of the five and is stuck dead at
chance, with the highest final action std of any of them -- the most room to
move. NatureCnn-SpatialSoftmax is a different encoder family and the one that
lost most to the colour DR, 0.553 down to 0.013, so it has the most to recover.

MORE VISUALGOAL VARIATIONS -- all keep the marker and both colour randomizations

Everything below runs on VisualGoal, VisualFree or VisualGrow, so the drawn
target and the object/goal colour DR are always present. All flag-only unless
marked. Add --dry-run to any sweep line to see the jobs first.

A. Sparse-bonus curriculum -- the one mechanism that sidesteps the dead zone.
   2 jobs.

   The at-goal bonus needs 0.90 overlap, which takes roughly 5 mm AND 5 degrees
   together, so it never fires early and the policy is trained by the dense term
   alone -- the term with no gradient at 180 degrees. Starting the threshold
   loose makes the sparse signal reachable while orientation is still bad, and it
   tightens to 0.90 over 4000 iterations. This is the only idea here that gives
   orientation a reward channel that is not the dense factor.

   bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualGoal --profile b200 \
     --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
     -- --success-threshold-start 0.5 \
        --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

B. Goal-yaw resolution curriculum.  2 jobs.

   Quantises the goal yaw to 4 angles and doubles each rung, 1500 iterations
   apart, with the full circle present throughout -- so unlike every distance
   curriculum it never starts the object near-optimal. Verified to need no
   registered curriculum term: the flag builds one.

   bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualGoal --profile b200 \
     --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
     -- --goal-yaw-levels 4 \
        --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

C. Slow the separation curriculum VisualGrow already has.  2 jobs.

   GrowStart was measured at a 9 cm cap by iteration 400 and 13 cm by 800, with
   yaw flat at chance the whole way -- it left the easy regime before anything
   could be learned in it. VisualGrow carries that curriculum, so the
   --separation-* flags apply to it and can hold the cap at its floor first.

   bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualGrow --profile b200 \
     --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
     -- --separation-pin-iterations 2000 --separation-ramp-iterations 2000 \
        --orientation-reward linear \
        --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

D. Near-goal starts with a wide yaw spread.  2 jobs.

   NearGoal starts a quarter of episodes 6-15 mm out with 5-20 degrees of yaw
   error. Widening that spread to 90 degrees puts the policy in states where the
   position term is already satisfied and only rotation is left, which is the
   state the dense reward handles worst.

   bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualFree --profile b200 \
     --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
     -- --near-goal-probability 0.35 --near-goal-yaw-spread-deg 90 \
        --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

E. Combine the two most promising.  2 jobs.

   Sparse-bonus curriculum plus the linear factor: a reachable sparse signal and
   a dense one with gradient everywhere. Two changes at once, so run it only
   after A and pair 1 have reported, or accept that a win is unattributable.

   bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualGoal --profile b200 \
     --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
     -- --success-threshold-start 0.5 --orientation-reward linear \
        --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

F. Goal-yaw range curriculum on a drawn-goal variant -- VisualSlow.  2 jobs.

   Registered now. VisualGoal with SlowGoal's schedule: the goal yaw is pinned
   for 3,000 iterations, then widens in eight 22.5-degree rungs, reaching the
   full circle at iteration 4,750 with 1,250 left to consolidate. The object's
   initial yaw stays uniform throughout, so rotating pays from the first
   episode -- the property that made this work where the relative-yaw variant,
   which started the object already at the goal orientation, did not.

   No drawn-goal variant has ever carried it: they all descend from Uniform,
   which drops the curriculum. It is also the one intervention measured to move
   yaw early rather than late, 0.892 rad by iteration 400 where GrowStart was
   still flat at chance. Of everything in this file this is the strongest single
   lever on orientation.

   Confirmed identical to SlowGoal's schedule, with the marker and both colour
   randomizations added:
     curriculum  goal_yaw_range, vertical_contact_force_weight
     stages      pinned to step 48000 (3000 it), 8 rungs, full circle at 4750 it

   bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualSlow --profile b200 \
     --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
     -- --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000

   And the same with the linear factor, if you want both levers on orientation
   at once -- the curriculum controls which goals appear, the factor controls
   whether there is a gradient once they do, so they are close to independent:

   bash cluster/submit_sweep.sh --variant VisualSlow --profile b200 \
     --arch "DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16,CompactVit-Flatten" \
     -- --orientation-reward linear \
        --agent.algorithm.gamma 0.996 --agent.max-iterations 6000
