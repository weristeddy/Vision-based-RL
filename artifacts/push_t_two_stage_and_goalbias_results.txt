VARIANT A -- THE TWO-STAGE WEIGHT WORKS, ON DinoV2
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DinoV2 with the linear factor reached yaw 0.958 and 7.2% episode success. Against
the right baseline -- VisualGoal on the same architecture, same full-circle goal,
no yaw curriculum, constant w=0.5 -- that is a large gain:

    VisualGoal LIN constant w=0.5   1.531
    VisualGoal ms  constant w=0.5   1.349 / 1.589
    VisualGoal RAMP w0->0.8 + ms    1.106
    VisualGoal RAMP w0->0.8 + LIN   0.958      <-- this batch

So your original idea is sound, and the earlier w=0.8 *resume* really was the
mechanism that broke it rather than the weight: run inside one job as a ramp, the
same endpoint improves yaw by 0.57 rad over the constant-weight control.

It does not beat the goal-yaw curriculum. VisualSlow LIN yawRANGE on the same
architecture is 0.582/0.674 across two seeds with 21-24% success. The ramp gets
about half that.

The thing I said to watch did not happen. Action std through the ramp:

    rew  arch                      std@2000  std@3000  std@4000  std@end
    LIN  DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16      0.20      0.18      0.17     0.15
    ms   DinoV2ViTS14-LocalGrid16      0.22      0.21      0.20     0.17
    LIN  CompactVit-Flatten            0.28      0.24      0.24     0.24
    ms   CompactVit-Flatten            0.25      0.20      0.19     0.19

It decays monotonically through the whole ramp. Nothing like the resume, where it
ran from 0.250 to the 1.0 ceiling by iteration 7000. Gradually changing the
reward under a live critic is simply a different thing from swapping it under a
stale one, which is what the resume did.

Yaw across the phases, DinoV2 LIN: 1.442 at iteration 2000 (end of the
position-only stage, still chance), 1.300 at 4000 (ramp complete), 0.958 at 5999.
So the orientation learning happens *after* the weight arrives at 0.8, in the
last 2000 iterations, and it was still improving at the end. That is the one
place more budget would obviously pay -- but per your constraint, a better use of
6000 is to start the ramp earlier.

CompactVit fails here too, at 1.517 and 1.574. Consistent with the appendix on
that encoder: 30 runs, 13 variants, never below 1.19 sustained.


VARIANT B -- BOTH SPREADS FAILED, AND THE 5-20 DEGREE NUMBER IS A TRAP
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Uniform spread: all four runs at 1.545-1.577 against a chance of 1.571. Exactly
nothing learned. Position sits at 0.015 m -- where the episode starts -- so the
policy is not even moving the T. This is the hazard flagged before submitting:
the position term begins at its maximum and can only fall once the T is touched,
so under both reward shapes doing nothing is locally optimal, and the linear
factor does not rescue it. The idea is dead, and it is dead for a reason that was
predicted rather than discovered.

The 5-20 degree arm looks like a triumph at yaw 0.218 and it is not. That arm
*starts* at a mean absolute yaw error of 0.218 rad: near_goal_yaw_range is
(0.087, 0.349) rad sampled as a magnitude with a random sign, mean 0.218. The
runs ended at 0.218 and 0.222. They held the starting condition and closed none
of it. Episode success 0.000 and 0.001, at_goal 0.000 and 0.001, and overlap
0.625 against the 0.90 the bonus needs.

So the correct reference for that arm is 0.218, not 1.571, and both runs are at
it. Chance, not success. Worth stating plainly because 0.218 next to a 1.571
chance line is the most misleading number in this batch.

The two LIN runs of this arm are the ones that crashed. Given both ms runs sat
exactly on their initial condition, they are not worth resubmitting.


WHY THE LAST TWO CRASHED, AND THE FIX
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Catastrophic error: error while writing PCH file: No space left on device
    -> Warp NVRTC compilation error 6 -> CUDA kernel build failed with error code 6

Raised from mjwarp.step inside Simulation.__init__, so before training started.

cluster/train.slurm already redirects Warp's kernel cache to the job-local disk
(WARP_CACHE_PATH=${RUNTIME_DIR}/warp, which is why the log shows
/data/local/jobs/1119012/warp/1.15.0). But Warp writes its NVRTC *precompiled
headers* to $TMPDIR, which is a different path and was never set -- the log shows
them going to /tmp/wp_pch_5uij71dk inside the container. Four ranks each
compiling a full module set filled it.

Fixed in cluster/train.slurm, cluster/evaluate.sh and cluster/view.sh: TMP_DIR is
now ${RUNTIME_DIR}/tmp, created alongside the warp and torchrunx dirs, and passed
in as TMPDIR, TMP and TEMP. RUNTIME_DIR is already bind-mounted, so no new bind
is needed. bash -n passes on all three.

Nothing about the two runs' configuration was wrong; resubmitting them unchanged
on the patched script would work. I would not bother, for the reason above.
